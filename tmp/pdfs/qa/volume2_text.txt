What this volume delivers
This volume keeps the two agreed projects and their shared monosemanticity motivation. It does not
replace them with a benchmark, an agent system or a different application area. It supplies a mathemat-
ically explicit foundation that can support their development.
The exact formulas delivered here include: operational perturbation radii; reconstruction risk; a finite-
state feature-detection risk; a small, nonlinear superposition phase boundary with feature frequency and
importance; a general interference-based error bound; exact recursive toy dynamics; a bound connecting
one-generation feature errors to cumulative distribution drift; a local derivative for a genuinely retrained
model; and a Gaussian denoising calculation clarifying the diffusion connection.
These are finished conditional calculations, not a claim that the proposed conference contribution is
already established. Some are standard facts presented with proofs; others are worked combinations
specialized to this program. Their novelty has not been certified. The general learned-geometry phase
diagram and a validated monosemanticity-based predictor in real diffusion models remain the research
targets.
That distinction is essential to using these notes productively. A theorem can be correct and still be too
elementary for a new paper. A promising experiment can still contradict a preferred mechanism. The
purpose of the derivations is to make the next eight weeks test precise claims, not to promise an outcome
that has not been observed.
The scientific question in plain language
When a model shares representational directions between concepts, it can store more information in
limited space. That sharing can also create confusion. We want to understand the conditions under
which the gain in coverage outweighs the cost in errors, and whether the same errors accumulate when
generated outputs become future training data.
This is a general learning-and-representation question. Safety is a possible application, but no adversary
is needed for the main study. Recursive training supplies the connection to the team’s self-improvement
interests; a self-consuming loop does not count as improvement unless quality actually improves. Vision
diffusion provides a real generative setting in which to test the mechanism.
Read these chapters in sequence
Chapters 1–3 establish definitions and repair the earlier formulas. Chapters 4–6 derive Project 1 results.
Chapters 7–10 derive Project 2 and the mathematical connection.
Chapters 11–13 explain diffusion,
controlled validation and the eight-week execution plan. The appendices give a formula ledger, exercises,
numerical results and primary references.
No team member is assigned ownership in this document. The work has a continuous theoretical and
experimental thread, with room for complementary interests in interpretation, robustness, evaluation,
post-training and generative modeling.

1.1
The commitments that remain fixed
Project 1 seeks a robust-performance tradeoff in sparsity, feature-to-dimension load and impor-

and a quantity that predicts it.
Their intended combination uses shared feature geometry,
shared toy infrastructure and a real-model demonstration. The source anchor is Zhang et al.’s
monosemanticity-and-robustness paper, taught in Volume I.
The mathematical choices below instantiate this direction; they do not exhaust it. We begin
with a task whose ground truth is unambiguous: recovering binary feature presence. This allows
an actual error probability to replace an informal “cleanness” score.

1.3
Three noise locations
1. Feature-space corruption: b or a continuous feature vector is perturbed before encod-
ing. A feature-space adversary is an idealized mathematical model.
2. Code-space corruption: h = Wb + ξ with ξ ∼N(0, σ2Im). This makes each matched
readout’s variance depend on Gii.
3. Observed-input corruption: pixels or waveforms change before a nonlinear encoder.
The encoder’s Jacobian and nonlinear activation changes matter.
These yield different formulas. A proof for the first cannot be described as a guarantee for the
third without an additional argument.

2.4
Answers to the earlier conceptual questions
Can an attacker move a perfectly isolated feature? Yes. If G = I, then Mi = 1, but
changing xi changes its reading. An ability to move the score does not imply Mi < 1.
Does a negative overlap count? In this unrestricted worst-case ℓ2 setting, Gij enters squared,
because an adversary can choose the harmful sign. For ordinary nonnegative data and a thresh-
old decoder, the sign can be beneficial or harmful depending on co-occurrence. The square loses
that distinction.
Does rarity determine Mi? No. At fixed G, Equation (2.3) has no activation probability
in it. Rarity may affect what G learns, or statistical collision probabilities, but it cannot be
inserted into this fixed-geometry identity by multiplication.
Is Mi scale invariant? It is invariant under a common rescaling W 7→aW, since all entries
of G scale by a2. Rescaling only one column changes its overlaps relative to its diagonal and
generally changes Mi. The diagonal has not disappeared physically merely because it is hidden
in normalized overlaps.

To obtain a finite-radius guarantee, bound the remainder or a Jacobian norm over the entire per-

The first term propagates a changed input distribution through the current decoder. The second
term captures how retraining changes the decoder itself. A fixed-G or fixed-K argument omits
this second mechanism.
At a smooth fixed point, eigenvalues of this Jacobian on the probability-simplex tangent space
provide a local stability criterion: spectral radius below one gives local asymptotic stability
under the usual finite-dimensional differentiability conditions. A stable fixed point can still be
far from P0, so quantify its fidelity separately.
10.4
A tractable staged experiment
Start with a small finite family of candidate geometries, such as isolated versus paired codes with
stated norm constraints. Evaluate the training objective exactly under Pg, select the minimizer
by a deterministic rule, and generate using its exact kernel. This yields a genuinely adaptive
piecewise-defined recursion that can be enumerated and checked.
Then move to learned continuous dictionaries. At each generation, retrain on sampled data and
compare observed one-step errors with the prediction conditional on the measured Gg. If the
geometry changes, report it. The goal is not to force a fixed-matrix prediction onto a changing
model.
10.5
Order parameters worth testing
Use a small set of quantities with distinct meanings: per-feature false negatives and false posi-
tives; reference-distribution feature retention; joint-state retention; normalized row leakage; de-
coder reconstruction fidelity; and the local transition or learning Jacobian when it is estimable.
Candidate predictors should be evaluated on later generations or held-out regimes, not selected
after seeing all collapse outcomes.
A basis-invariant covariance rank measures occupied linear directions. It does not by itself iden-
tify named concepts. A neuron purity measure depends on a semantic reference. Neither should
be relabeled as the other. The research opportunity is to show which geometric and semantic
measurements add predictive information beyond frequency, norm, ordinary reconstruction error
and training loss.
Status of this result. Equations (10.4)–(10.5) are conditional local derivations.
Solving a global nonlinear learning recursion and proving that a particular semantic
scalar forecasts real-model collapse remain research work.

Two clocks, two different processes

12.7
Interventions that support a mechanism
An intervention should change the hypothesized interference while monitoring clean quality,
norms and coverage. Examples include a controlled coherence penalty in a toy model, a sparsity-
promoting module, or a targeted direction manipulation with a random-direction control. A
penalty that orthogonalizes everything may simply reduce stored information, so compare at
matched capacity and inspect feature loss.
The strongest evidence is a linked change: the intervention alters measured geometry, changes
the predicted one-step errors and changes later feature retention in the predicted direction. A
correlation between low purity and late generations alone is much weaker.
12.8
Evaluation and uncertainty
Report training seeds as independent replications. For rare events, provide sample counts and
uncertainty; zero observed events is not proof of zero probability. If a concept has true frequency
p, a sample of size N contains none with probability (1 −p)N, approximately e−Np. To make
this probability at most δ, one needs roughly N ≥log(1/δ)/p. This calculation belongs in the
sampling plan.
For generative quality, combine a distributional image metric with concept-frequency and joint-
frequency measures. Aggregate metrics can miss tail loss. Use held-out real reference data for
evaluation, and never select thresholds or order parameters on the final test outcomes without
reporting that selection.

If real data have mean µ0 and covariance C0, while generated data have mean µQ and covariance

B.5
Exercise 3: why a largest overlap and a sum differ
Compare one nuisance with overlap a and four nuisances with overlap a/2. Their total squared
overlap is equal. Under isotropic unrestricted feature noise, the readout variance can agree.
Under sparse nonnegative activations, their distributions of interference differ: one large jump
is not the same random variable as a sum of four smaller jumps. The Bernstein bound records
both Vi and Bi to reflect this distinction.
B.6
Exercise 4: solve the mixed fixed kernel
Starting from Pg+1 = αP0+(1−α)KPg, repeatedly substitute the previous step to obtain a finite
geometric series plus (1 −α)gKgP0. The final term vanishes for α > 0 in the probability-vector
norm, leaving the fixed-point series in Chapter 8. This is a statement about repeated use of a
fixed channel; refitting generally changes K and prevents that same closed form.
B.7
Exercise 5: covariance does not identify concepts
Take a two-dimensional isotropic Gaussian and rotate it by 45 degrees. The covariance and
distribution are unchanged, but coordinate axes are different.
A covariance-based criterion
alone cannot say which axes correspond to meaningful concepts. Non-Gaussian planted factors
or external semantic measurements are necessary for that question.
B.8
Exercise 6: a sharply stated falsification
Suppose an SAE overlap score predicts late-generation image degradation until reconstruction
error and feature frequency are included, after which it adds no held-out predictive information.
This does not invalidate the exact toy algebra. It weakens the claim that the chosen geometry
measure explains real-model collapse beyond simpler measurements. The paper should report
and analyze that distinction rather than call the transfer confirmed.
