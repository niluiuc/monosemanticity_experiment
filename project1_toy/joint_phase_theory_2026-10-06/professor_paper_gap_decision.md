# Professor decision: strongest claim and remaining paper gap

6 October 2026. Written before the fixed vision pilot outcome. No optional toy cases are recommended.

## Actual strongest claim

**A representation's mono-versus-sharing reconstruction-robustness boundary depends quantitatively on the decoder evaluation policy, even when the clean-trained encoder, task, corruption and comparison budget are fixed.** Clean storage geometry alone does not determine the observed risk ordering.

The clean-critical importance family supplies a proved storage transition and asymptotic boundary with different coefficients under frozen and symmetrically calibrated biases. The eta2/3 endpoint makes the distinction particularly sharp: on the same critical noise scale, identical clean-selected sharing geometry beats mono with frozen biases while mono wins under symmetric calibration. The mechanism is the relative placement of decoder gates and the calibration benefit at mono's zero gate.

This is more precise than the already familiar qualitative claim that superposition can improve clean capacity and harm robustness. The basic toy architecture, Gram geometry and Gaussian ReLU moments are established tools, not claimed inventions. The candidate contribution is their training-selected, globally justified critical comparison and its evaluation-policy consequence. A literature audit must still check that exact claim; these proofs alone do not establish novelty.

## Proved versus observed

- Proved within the declared Bernoulli two-feature/one-code-dimensional model: global clean selection across both signs, importance assignments and scalar bias regions; clean birth amplitude and cubic gain; uniform critical laws on the stated importance rectangle; endpoint global selection on .495<=p<.5; different frozen/calibrated coefficients and asymptotic opposite ordering.
- Numerical validation: prescribed finite geometries and calibration ledgers, root brackets and independent formula/ledger audits. High-precision arithmetic with declared slack is not a directed-rounding certificate. Detected crossings do not certify a universal crossing count.
- Not established: arbitrary-load trained networks, semantic monosemanticity as recovered by SAEs, robustness to image-input corruption or adversaries, the same exponent in real activations, recursive training stability, or a complete broad mono/share phase diagram. The nonconverged eight-feature arm cannot fill these gaps.

## What the fixed vision pilot can supply

If eligible and resolved, it tests the decoder-policy mechanism on genuine learned nonnegative activations with continuous amplitudes and dependence rather than Bernoulli inputs. It separates noise calibration from ordinary train/calibration mismatch and uses held-out conditional risk. This is a real learned-feature transfer, but a two-channel reconstruction bottleneck remains a narrow operational example.

Arbitrary ResNet channels are not known semantic concepts. Coordinate-retaining mono means one operational activation per retained code direction in this task; it does not prove that a full ResNet neuron is monosemantic. A positive policy contrast must not be reported as a full-model semantic interpretability gain or a general contradiction of monosemanticity-helps-robustness.

## Stopping decision after the five-level pilot

- If inputs, zero support, mixed clean selection or optimization gates fail: stop this pilot and report it non-diagnostic. Do not search channels/backbones/weights until an attractive example appears.
- If eligible but policy comparison is negative or unresolved: retain the result and stop the fixed run. It bounds the empirical scope; it does not justify claiming transfer or changing tolerances.
- If eligible and a resolved policy contrast/opposite ordering appears: first independently reproduce the identical saved-input computation. The next necessary step is to establish whether the observed gate/calibration asymmetry actually explains the contrast, using the **already saved** per-image loss changes and clean decoder gate-distance/zero-support records. This is a targeted mechanism audit, not another parameter search.

Only if that mechanism audit supports transfer and the intended paper explicitly claims **semantic** monosemanticity should an additional semantic-linked demonstration be considered. The smallest useful version is one independently identified visual concept pair from an existing annotated activation/SAE resource, selected before robustness evaluation, with known concept association and the same frozen/calibrated comparison. It requires genuine labels or prior feature evidence and a predefined pair; arbitrary channel names or an automatic label generated after the result are insufficient. If such a resource and interpretation are unavailable within the time budget, narrow the paper's claim to operational feature packing and decoder-policy robustness instead of launching a new SAE or semantic benchmark project.

The remaining paper-critical tasks are exact-claim novelty assessment, a faithful compact proof narrative, independently audited numerical support, and a clear learned-feature scope statement. Strong main-track ambition requires evidence and significance beyond a mathematically correct special case; no acceptance or novelty promise follows from this decision.
